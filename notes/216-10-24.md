# [[216,10,24]] — weight-6 generalized bicycle on $\mathbb{Z}_{18} \times \mathbb{Z}_6$

## Direction & hypothesis

This candidate targets the unrestricted / weight-9plus operational frontier
with a generalized-bicycle construction. The code was retained from an earlier
search because its moderate blocklength and ten logical qubits offered a
different rate-distance tradeoff from the larger constant-rate entries.

## What was searched

The construction is a two-block group-algebra code on the abelian group
$\mathbb{Z}_{18} \times \mathbb{Z}_6$ (group order 108), with weight-6 supports

```text
a = [33, 105, 12, 17, 76, 70]
b = [88, 25, 107, 97, 72, 53]
```

The original staged claim was $d \le 26$. A deeper independent RIS check at
20,000 trials per side found weight-24 logicals on both sides, so that claim
was corrected before submission.

## Evidence trail

The corrected package contains explicit weight-24 X and Z logical witnesses.
At 50,000 RIS trials per side, the lightest found weight was 24 on both X and
Z. At 200,000 RIS trials per side, again using seed `20260828`, the result was
unchanged: X = 24 and Z = 24, with no lighter logical found.

The final claim is therefore the witness-backed upper bound $d \le 24$, not an
exact-distance certification. The trusted validator passes CSS, $n=216$,
$k=10$, and the weight-9plus / unrestricted track classification. It also
reports no exact duplicate or WL-equivalent entry and `board_advancing: true`.
The operational figure is $kd^2/n = 10\cdot24^2/216 \approx 26.67$.

## Dead ends

The initial $d \le 26$ claim was an example of distance inflation at a lower
search depth. The 20,000-trial run refuted it with weight-24 witnesses on both
sides. No exact certification was attempted.
