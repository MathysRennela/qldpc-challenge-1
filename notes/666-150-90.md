# [[666,150,90]] designed-divisor generalized-bicycle code

## Direction & hypothesis

The search targeted a high-rate unrestricted generalized-bicycle construction over the cyclic group `Z_333`. The hypothesis was that keeping a degree-75 common divisor of `x^333+1` would preserve the `k=150` rank regime while allowing sparse output words with balanced supports.

## What was searched

The campaign factored `x^333+1` over GF(2), recovered the degree-75 common divisor from the construction seed, and sampled bounded XOR combinations of cyclic shifts of two sparse words already in that ideal. Candidate pairs were reduced under cyclic translation, built as exact CSS matrices, rank-filtered, and screened with randomized information-set searches. The generation and packaging used the repository's research kit and is described by the exact parameters in the reproduction section below.

The submitted supports are the 14-term and 16-term words recorded in the JSON provenance. The resulting matrices have `n=666`, `k=150`, and maximum row weight 30.

## Evidence trail

The research-kit submission path computed and embedded both logical witnesses. The stored side witnesses have weights X=90 and Z=94, so the claim is the witness-backed upper bound `d<=90`; no exact-distance claim is made.

A fresh 2,500-trial-per-side Python RIS check at seed `20260818` found weight 90 and no lighter logical. The result is retained as limited corroboration, not a proof. The complete output is recorded in the local research record; the trusted validator independently accepted the staged submission's structure, CSS relations, dimensions, witnesses, and current-board status.

## Dead ends

Low-weight ordinary multiplier enumeration produced no useful degree-75 words under the target row-weight cap. The search therefore used sparse-output XOR combinations of cyclic shifts instead. Candidates below the threshold were not promoted as finalists.

## Tools

The construction used the repository's `group_algebra.py`, `css.py`, `surrogate.py`, `submit.py`, and trusted validator. All arithmetic is over GF(2). The reported distance remains an upper bound from explicit witnesses and randomized refutation searches.

## Reproduction

For `N=333`, use the cyclic generalized-bicycle construction

```text
H_X = [circ(A) | circ(B)]
H_Z = [circ(B)^T | circ(A)^T]
```

with

```text
A = [0, 2, 20, 49, 58, 128, 139, 146, 157, 200, 222, 238, 247, 281]
B = [0, 1, 17, 37, 58, 93, 107, 136, 147, 149, 152, 165, 260, 273, 324, 326]
```

where `circ(S)` is the binary `333 x 333` circulant whose row `i` has support `(i+j) mod 333` for `j` in `S`. The complete expanded checks and witnesses are in `codes/666-150-90.json`.
