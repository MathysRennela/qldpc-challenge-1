# [[666,150,88]] designed-divisor generalized-bicycle code

## Direction & hypothesis

The search targeted a high-rate unrestricted generalized-bicycle construction over `Z_333`. The construction keeps the same degree-75 common-divisor mechanism while replacing one sparse ideal word and retaining a second sparse word. The hypothesis was that a nearby support mutation could retain `k=150` and improve the witnessed minimum logical weight.

## What was searched

The campaign factored `x^333+1` over GF(2), recovered the degree-75 common divisor from the construction seed, and sampled bounded XOR combinations of cyclic shifts of sparse words in that ideal. Candidate pairs were translated to exact CSS matrices, checked for rank and commutation, and screened with randomized information-set searches. The generation and packaging used the repository's research kit and is described by the exact parameters in the reproduction section below.

The submitted supports are the 14-term and 16-term words recorded in the JSON provenance. The resulting matrices have `n=666`, `k=150`, and maximum row weight 30.

## Evidence trail

The research-kit submission path computed and embedded both logical witnesses. A fresh Python RIS search found a Z-side logical of weight 88, and the candidate was repackaged with that witness. The claim is therefore the witness-backed upper bound `d<=88`; no exact-distance claim is made.

The corrected submission passed the trusted validator, including CSS commutation, dimensions, row weights, witness validity, and the current-board checks. The validator's refutation pass found no logical lighter than the stored claim in its bounded run. This is evidence for staging only, not a proof of the exact distance.

## Dead ends

The original screening estimate for this support pair was higher, but a fresh refutation found the weight-88 Z logical. The candidate was corrected rather than discarded so the lower-weight witness remains part of the evidence trail. Low-weight ordinary multiplier enumeration produced no useful degree-75 words under the target row-weight cap, so the campaign used sparse-output XOR combinations of cyclic shifts.

## Tools

The construction used the repository's `group_algebra.py`, `css.py`, `surrogate.py`, `submit.py`, and trusted validator. All arithmetic is over GF(2). The reported distance is an explicit-witness upper bound and is not exact.

## Reproduction

For `N=333`, use

```text
H_X = [circ(A) | circ(B)]
H_Z = [circ(B)^T | circ(A)^T]
```

with

```text
A = [3, 73, 84, 91, 102, 145, 167, 183, 192, 226, 278, 280, 298, 327]
B = [0, 1, 17, 37, 58, 93, 107, 136, 147, 149, 152, 165, 260, 273, 324, 326]
```

where `circ(S)` is the binary `333 x 333` circulant whose row `i` has support `(i+j) mod 333` for `j` in `S`. The complete expanded checks and witnesses are in `codes/666-150-88.json`.
