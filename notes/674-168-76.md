# [[674,168,76]] — cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a degree-84 ideal

Author: @vprusso. The submitted distance is a witnessed upper bound, **d <= 80**,
with maximum check weight 30. The score using that bound is
`168 * 80^2 / 674 = 1595.2522255193`, 9.511% above the comparison entry
`[[682,172,76]]` at 1456.7038123167. This is not an exact-distance claim;
literature novelty is unverified.

## Direction & hypothesis

The target was the weight-9plus × unrestricted frontier. In a cyclic
generalized-bicycle code, the common polynomial gcd controls the number of
logical qubits, while the particular sparse generators also affect mixed
logical operators. Order 337 offers sixteen degree-21 irreducible factors.
Selecting four gives a degree-84 ideal and potentially k=168; it changes the
constituent code rather than transporting a known low-weight constituent
witness into another presentation of the same ideal.

## What was searched

Products of four degree-21 factors were grouped under the unit multipliers
modulo 337, giving 116 ideal orbits. One orbit already represented on the board
was excluded. A fixed 120-second constructor budget visited 30 remaining
ideals and recovered four distinct sparse words, one in each of four ideals.
The shared NumPy RNG seed was 928300001. The constructor formed the binary
rowspace of all cyclic shifts of each generator, permuted its columns,
computed RREF with the native GF(2) core, and retained rows of weight 4 to 18.
Words with an additional gcd factor other than x+1 were rejected. Translation
and Frobenius images of a retained word were deduplicated.

The submitted word came from catalog index 21 at local RREF iteration 5002,
within 6690 iterations and approximately four seconds allocated to that ideal.
The timed catalog shares one RNG stream; the seed and local iteration describe
discovery, not a timing-independent replay recipe. The supports below give an
exact reconstruction independently of discovery timing.

Each recovered word A was paired with its relative Frobenius image B=A^4
modulo x^337+1. Four codes were packaged with one initial witness-search trial
per side, then audited using constrained fixed spaces and native searches.
The final admission check excluded the weight-34 and weight-36 candidates.

## Dead ends

The same four-factor batch produced a k=170, weight-36 candidate whose bound
fell from 84 to 80 over 500,000 trials per side, and a k=168, weight-34 candidate
that fell from 84 to 78 after 200,000 trials per side. Both violate the
challenge's maximum check weight of 32 and were rejected by the unchanged
admission checks; their numeric scores are not admissible leaderboard results.
The fourth candidate, also weight 36, was additionally reduced to 76 by the
j=7 fixed-space audit. Future construction screens should run the complete
structural admission check before spending a deep distance budget.

Earlier three-factor ideals at the same order produced four shallow survivors
with bounds 96, 92, 90 and 94. Fresh 100,000-trial general searches at pair depth
64 reduced them to 88, 88, 87 and 89. These collapses motivated both the deeper
general ladder and the fixed-space audits used here.

## Reproduction

Work over F_2[x]/(x^337+1). Let C(S) be the 337 by 337 binary circulant whose
row i has ones at `(i+s) mod 337` for s in S. Use:

```text
A = [14, 23, 27, 44, 45, 50, 113, 177, 185, 212, 222, 247, 251, 271, 305]
B = [34, 56, 66, 73, 92, 108, 115, 174, 176, 180, 200, 209, 214, 314, 330]
H_X = [C(A) | C(B)]
H_Z = [C(B)^T | C(A)^T]
```

B is obtained by multiplying every A exponent by 4 modulo 337. The X-check
with row index i therefore contains `(i+a) mod 337` for a in A and
`337+(i+b) mod 337` for b in B. The Z-check contains `(i-b) mod 337` for b in B
and `337+(i-a) mod 337` for a in A. This specifies every submitted matrix entry.
The commuting circulants imply the CSS commutation identity. Each matrix has
337 rows, 674 columns and rank 253; consequently k=674-253-253=168. Every check
has weight 15+15=30.

For an algebraic cross-check, encode a binary polynomial f by the integer
`sum(f_i * 2^i)`. The four irreducible factor bitmasks are:

```text
2332367, 2437221, 2932169, 3612295
```

Their product over F_2 is
`g84 = 33893924722822194880126641`. Both
`gcd(A(x), x^337+1)` and `gcd(B(x), x^337+1)` equal g84, of degree 84.
The parity extension `(x+1)*g84` has bitmask
`43653333958895553830659027` and degree 85; it is not a factor of either chosen
15-term word. Thus the submitted pair uses g84, not the parity-extended ideal.
The explicit matrices and logical supports are in `codes/674-168-76.json`.
