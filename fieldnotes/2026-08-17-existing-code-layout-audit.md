## Objective

Audit existing board entries that have a credible geometric origin but no
committed layout. The purpose is to recover locality-track records and create
reliable inputs for later geometric-efficiency searches, not to assign
coordinates indiscriminately.

## Hypothesis

Some periodic, twisted-torus, planar, or product constructions already have a
natural embedding implicit in their construction. Reconstructing those
coordinates may produce a locality-track advance even when the code is
uncompetitive on the unrestricted board.

## Candidate selection

Prioritize entries with:

- an explicit torus, quotient, planar, or translational construction;
- checks generated by bounded displacements or a published cellulation;
- a plausible single-layer or bilayer coordinate rule;
- enough distance and logical rate for locality membership to matter.

Deprioritize arbitrary nonabelian high-rate matrices with no geometric action,
and any embedding that requires a box whose diameter grows like `sqrt(n)` while
checks remain long-range.

## First experiment

1. Select 10--20 existing codes from distinct construction descriptions.
2. Reconstruct coordinates from the construction, paper data, or a committed
   generator; do not optimize coordinates freely without recording the rule.
3. Run the submission builder and trusted validator for each reconstructed
   layout.
4. Record the verifier-derived locality class, interaction radius, layer
   count, spacing, density, and geometric efficiency.
5. Check exact and WL-equivalent duplicates and compare every nested track's
   Pareto relation.
6. Keep successful reconstructions as candidate artifacts for human review;
   do not alter `codes/` during the unattended campaign.

## Success criteria

The primary success is a validated locality-track advance. Secondary successes
are:

- a new local Pareto point from a code previously visible only on the
  unrestricted board;
- a trustworthy layout reconstruction that calibrates a later family search;
- an honest measurement showing that a natural layout fails the radius cap or
  receives negligible `g`.

A layout is not successful merely because it is accepted as unrestricted.

## Stop conditions

Stop a candidate family after its natural layouts either fail the verifier's
spacing/radius requirements, duplicate existing entries, or provide no local
Pareto advance. Do not replace a failed construction with arbitrary cramming or
long-range coordinate compression.

## Evidence to preserve

For every attempt preserve the source construction, coordinate-generation
rule, complete coordinates, layers, measured radius and spacing diagnostics,
check weights, distance confidence, duplicate result, and validator verdict.
If the layout comes from external literature, pin the public source and version;
never cite an uncommitted local or ignored artifact as durable evidence.

## Decision

This audit is now the next active campaign after the cross-breed lead was parked
as a repeatedly exhausted provenance dead end. No layout reconstruction result
is recorded yet; begin with a small, construction-driven sample and preserve
natural coordinate rules, verifier diagnostics, and all failed results. Do not
use arbitrary coordinate cramming.

## First audit results — 2026-08-20

Two natural-layout reconstructions were generated and passed the structural
verifier, but neither passed the promotion gate:

- `[[108,8,d<=10]]` bivariate bicycle on `Z_9 × Z_6`, using quotient coordinates
  on two layers: valid coordinates, but verifier locality was `unrestricted`;
  the artifact was an exact duplicate of `108-8-10.json` and dominated by
  `[[90,8,d<=10]]`.
- `[[104,30,d<=8]]` pair-partition CPM with lift `p=13` and eight block columns,
  using lift/block-column coordinates: valid coordinates, but interaction radius
  was `13.8924`, so locality was `unrestricted`; the artifact was an exact
  duplicate of `104-30-8.json`.

These are calibration records, not board submissions; their ignored local
artifacts are not durable evidence. The durable result is that both natural
coordinate rules passed structural verification but failed locality/novelty
promotion. The next sample must use a construction with a stronger intrinsic
bounded-displacement geometry, such as a twisted-torus or published
planar/cellulation code; do not keep resubmitting periodic layouts that produce
long-range wraparound checks.

The twisted-torus `[[114,4,d<=14]]` entry was inspected next. Its provenance
pins the quotient lattice and polynomials, but no committed generator or
qubit-index-to-quotient-element labeling is present. The first check rows
confirm that raw qubit indices are a nontrivial permutation of quotient
translations. Assigning coordinates from the raw indices would therefore be
arbitrary cramming, so this candidate is recorded as a reproduction blocker,
not evaluated as a locality result. Do not revisit it unless a pinned labeling
or source generator becomes available.
